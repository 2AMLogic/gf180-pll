- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - **The bound being tested**, quoted from the record that established it
    (`sim/pll-top-smoke/records/20260801-085349-0e5c22d.md`): `design/edgedet.sch`
    fires each of the PFD's SR latches from `AND(X, NOT(X delayed by 5
    inverters))`; that set pulse measures **0.33-0.39 ns**. An integrator whose
    mean internal step is comparable to that width steps clean over the set
    pulse on a large fraction of feedback edges; each miss is an edge the PFD
    never sees, and the loop then reads as **jammed with UP asserted, ramping
    Vctrl to the rail while the feedback is ALREADY faster than the reference**.
    100 ps puts 3-4 internal steps inside the set pulse; a 500 ps ceiling was
    measured missing 9 of 16 feedback edges.
  - **How the violation arose (mechanism, not carelessness)**: both #12 decks
    wrote `.tran {tstep} {tstop}` with **no 4th argument**. ngspice then
    defaults the ceiling on the *internal* timestep to the *print* step. Both
    `run.sh`s size that print step as `1/(50*f_out)` -- a sensible choice for a
    print step and a meaningless one for an integration ceiling, because
    `f_out` has nothing to do with the PFD's set pulse. The two quantities were
    never distinguished, so nobody chose a bad ceiling; the ceiling was an
    unintended side effect of a print-step decision. That is also why the fix
    is structural (one shared constant, passed as an explicit 4th argument)
    rather than a corrected number in two places.
  - **Measurement method**: each cited record committed a `wrdata` waveform
    CSV. `wrdata` dumps the vectors ngspice holds after `run`, which for a
    transient are the **accepted internal timepoints**, not the print grid.
    Verified rather than assumed: the `typical` relock CSV has 8701 rows over a
    2 us window (the print grid at 250 ps would be ~8000 points) and a minimum
    step of **1.62 ps**, three orders of magnitude below the print step -- a
    print grid cannot produce that. The committed artifacts
    therefore contain a direct, auditable record of the integrator's actual
    step sequence, and the table below is measured from them rather than
    inferred from the deck.
  - **Extraction validated against its source record before being trusted**:
    re-deriving the headline numbers of `20260801-073931-eec269e` from its own
    CSV reproduces them -- `vctrl_final` = **3.13821 V** (that record states
    3.138207 V) and an `up` duty of **86.7%** (it states 86.5%; the 0.2 pt
    difference is the digital-threshold convention, 1.65 V here). The
    extraction is reading the same quantities that record read.
  - **Limitation -- what this record does NOT establish**: it does **not**
    show what `sim/lock-time`'s `relock` transient does at a compliant ceiling.
    It shows that the ceiling was violated, by how much, and that the violation
    is of the kind #52 demonstrated can invert the verdict. Whether the
    `relock` rail excursion survives at 100 ps is an open question this record
    deliberately leaves open rather than guesses at. See below.

- **Result**:

  ### 1. Measured internal-timestep compliance (from committed artifacts)

  | Campaign / artifact | Ceiling in force | Mean internal step | Max step | Steps longer than the 0.33 ns set pulse | Steps per set pulse | Bound satisfied? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `lock-time` relock, `typical`/27C/3.30V | 250 ps (= print step, 1/(50 x 80 MHz)) | **0.230 ns** | 0.250 ns | 0 of 8700 (0.0%) | ~1.5 | **NO** (2.5x over) |
  | `lock-time` relock, `ss`/125C/2.97V | 250 ps | **0.232 ns** | 0.250 ns | 0 of 8610 (0.0%) | ~1.5 | **NO** (2.5x over) |
  | `output-range` `lo` edge, `typical`/27C/3.30V | 2 ns (= print step, 1/(50 x 10 MHz)) | **1.756 ns** | 2.0 ns | **1037 of 1139 (91.0%)** | ~0.2 | **NO** (20x over) |
  | `output-range` `hi` edge, `typical`/27C/3.30V | 100 ps (1/(50 x 200 MHz)) | no waveform committed | -- | -- | >=3.5 (ceiling-derived) | incidentally **yes** |

  Two things in this table deserve stating plainly rather than leaving to the
  reader:

  1. **The integrator was pinned at the ceiling, not choosing its own step.**
     Mean/max is 0.92 and 0.93 for the two `lock-time` rows and 0.88 for
     `output-range`'s `lo`. The step was being set by the ceiling, not by
     local truncation
     error -- which is precisely the condition under which tightening the
     ceiling changes the answer rather than merely refining it.
  2. **`output-range`'s `lo` edge is the severe case, and it is worse than the
     case #52 already demonstrated to be fatal.** 91% of its internal steps are
     longer than the PFD's *entire* set pulse. #52 showed a 500 ps ceiling
     (mean step 0.38 ns) missing 9 of 16 feedback edges; `lo` ran at a mean
     step of 1.756 ns, roughly 4.6x coarser again.

  `hi` complying is a coincidence, not a design choice: `1/(50 x 200 MHz)`
  happens to equal 100 ps exactly, while the same formula gives 2 ns at 10 MHz.
  That the identical formula lands 20x apart across one campaign's two rows is
  the clearest available demonstration that it was never a bound.

  ### 2. Which committed claims this qualifies

  Per `sim/README.md`'s append-only rule, nothing below is edited or retracted.
  These records stand as committed; this record qualifies how to read them.

  - **`sim/lock-time/records/20260801-073931-eec269e.md`** (and its
    second-corner extension `20260801-091429-5fd8489.md`) concluded that the
    `relock` `vctrl_final` rail excursion is *"a genuine, reproducible
    large-frequency-error PFD/CP acquisition-dynamics anomaly (`up` dominates
    86.5% of the window despite the VCO free-running faster than the reference
    -- the opposite of the naive tri-state-PFD expectation)."*
    **That empirical signature is, phrase for phrase, the artefact signature
    #52 documents**: *"the loop then reads as jammed with UP asserted, ramping
    Vctrl to the rail while the feedback is ALREADY faster than the reference
    ... entirely an artefact of the integration."* Both records reached their
    conclusion at a 250 ps ceiling, 2.5x over the bound, which had not yet
    landed and so was not available to them.
    Those records' **observations stand** -- their waveforms are real and are
    re-measured above. Their shared **interpretation**, that the mechanism is
    loop/PFD dynamics rather than integration, is **not established** and must
    be re-tested at a compliant ceiling before it is relied on. Note this is a
    *coincidence of signature*, not proof of artefact: the two hypotheses
    predict the same qualitative waveform, which is exactly why only a
    compliant-ceiling re-run can separate them. Their own "What this record
    does NOT resolve" field already flagged the transistor-level mechanism as
    open; this is a second, independent reason to keep it open.
    The `ss`/125C/2.97V corner is worth flagging for whoever re-runs: its
    polarity is *reversed* (`dn` duty 79.5%, `vctrl_final` 2.300 V) rather than
    absent, which neither hypothesis obviously predicts.
  - **`sim/output-range/records/20260801-061907-67d7127.md`**'s `lo`-edge
    conclusion (ordinary large-signal charge-pump acquisition current,
    correctly signed) rests on a 2 ns-ceiling transient in which the PFD's set
    pulse was unresolved on 91% of steps. It is the least reliable of the three
    and should be re-run first among them.
  - **`sim/lock-time/records/20260731-221408-640560e.md`** (the original pilot)
    and `sim/output-range/records/20260731-223426-640560e.md` used the same
    decks with the same absent 4th argument, so the same qualification applies
    to their `relock` / `lo` rows.
  - **Not affected**: `sim/pll-top-smoke` (#52) set the bound and complies.
    Open-loop campaigns (`vco-tuning-range`, `pfd-deadzone`, `cp-compliance`,
    `lock-detector`, `divider-ratio`, `devchar-*`) contain no closed
    PFD-in-loop path and do not inherit this bound.

  ### 3. Controlled pair: attempted, not obtained

  The intended confirmatory experiment was a **controlled pair** -- the same
  `relock` corner (`typical`/27 C/3.30 V, N=4) run twice in this session, on
  one freshly assembled DUT, with **only** the internal-timestep ceiling
  differing (250 ps vs 100 ps), via the `SIM_TMAX` override added to
  `sim/lib/simenv.sh` for exactly this purpose. A pair, rather than a re-run
  compared against the committed 3.138 V, because the DUT hash, the simulator
  (42 -> 46), its linear solver (KLU -> SPARSE 1.3), the schematic-capture
  version and the host architecture have all moved since that record, so a
  single re-run's difference could be attributed to any of them.

  **Both arms were launched and both were abandoned incomplete.** They are
  reported here rather than omitted, because the reason is itself the
  operational finding #65 keeps re-encountering:

  - Host load average rose from **42 to 130** over the ~25 minutes the two runs
    were up, with ~52 concurrent `ngspice` processes from sibling agent
    sessions in other worktrees.
  - The two runs were each receiving **4-5% of one core**. In the final 9.5
    minutes of wall clock they accumulated ~28 CPU-seconds *between them*.
  - Neither reached its transient's end; neither produced a `vctrl_final`
    reading, a waveform CSV, or any measurement. **No number from either arm
    appears anywhere in this record**, and no `corners/` directory or netlist
    snapshot is minted for this record ID.

  This is the third consecutive time this campaign has lost a run to host
  contention (see `20260801-073931-eec269e.md`'s throughput note: 714.3 CPU-s
  contended vs 62.1 CPU-s idle for the same corner and window). Abandoning was
  the correct call over grinding: #65's own guidance is to scale down or defer
  rather than spend the budget producing timing-corrupted data.

  **The static analysis in sections 1-2 does not depend on this pair.** It is
  measured entirely from committed artifacts and stands on its own; the pair
  would have converted "the bound was violated, by a margin #52 showed can
  invert the verdict" into "the verdict does / does not in fact invert here."
  That question is **open**, and is now the first thing #65's remaining items
  should resolve -- on an uncontended host.
