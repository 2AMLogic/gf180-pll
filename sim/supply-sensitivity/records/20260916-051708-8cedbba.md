  **Answer, and it moves the finding rather than confirming or clearing it:
  the 1.796 ns figure does not support the sentence built on it, and two

  state. Fifteen of the seventeen rows over the ratified bound are in that
  same condition, and every one of them is moving *downward*. The remaining
  two are settled, and they are the rows that actually put the loop outside
  the ratified criterion:

  | Corner | settled static phase | moved across the window | two-node cross-check |

  | `typical`/−40 °C/3.63 V | **1.049 ns** | −0.0106 ns | 2.26 % |

  So the answer to "is `<= 1 ns` met" is **no, but not where the spec says**,
  and the answer to "is 1.796 ns the worst-case static offset" is **it is not a
  static offset at all**. Neither conclusion relaxes the criterion; both change
  which evidence stands behind it. The decision record this backs is
  `spec/decision-records/DR-012-static-phase-offset-evidence.md`.

- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - **The criterion applied here is `spec/pll.md`'s ratified one, cited, not a
    proxy**: `|static phase error at the PFD inputs| <= 1 ns`, an absolute
    bound in seconds with no reference to f_ref. The source record's `verdict`
    column applied `ACC_PHI_FRAC = 0.02` of a reference period instead
    (1.6 ns at 12.5 MHz, 3.2 ns at the 6.25 MHz power-split bundle), which is
    1.6× and 3.2× looser. Both are reported per row (`over_ratified`,
    `over_deck`) so the gap is visible in the data rather than only in prose.
    The source record's own `verdict` column is reproduced unchanged beside
    them; **nothing in that record is restated or re-judged.**
  - **Is `phi_b` a settled static offset? The deck answers this itself, and
    the answer was never read.** `tb_supply_lock.sp` measures the REF→FB skew
    twice, at `ta` = 6.4 µs and `tb` = 9.6 µs, and reports

    ```
    ferr = -(phi_b - phi_a) / (tb - ta)
    ```

    because a residual fractional frequency error slips the phase at exactly
    df/f seconds per second. That identity inverts exactly:
    `phi_a = phi_b + ferr·(tb − ta)`, with no model and no new simulation. So
    every committed row carries, in two columns already present, how far its
    "static" phase moved between the run's own two instants. A row that moved
    by a large fraction of the bound it is being judged against was not
    measured in steady state.
  - **Why so many rows are unsettled: the window sits at one time constant.**
    `run.sh` runs the grid to 12.0 µs with the late window at 6.4/9.6 µs,
    which its own header states are 0.69 and 1.03 of the loop's slowest time
    constant (9.3 µs = 1/(2πRC1)). One τ is not settled, and the runner's
    escalation to 36.8 µs (3.96 τ) exists for exactly this -- but it was gated
    on `|ferr| > ACC_FERR = 1e-3` **only**. `ff`/125 °C/3.63 V has
    `ferr` = 2.255e-4, comfortably inside that gate, while its phase was
    moving 0.722 ns per window. The frequency gate is blind to the phase
    criterion because a small residual frequency error integrated over a long
    window is a large phase shift. (`run.sh` now gates on both -- see Links.)
  - **The settled/unsettled split is drawn at a tenth of the criterion**
    (|Δφ| across the window > 0.1 ns ⇒ not settled). The threshold is stated
    before the data rather than chosen from it, and it is relative to the
    bound the row is judged against rather than to the row's own magnitude, so
    it does not become more permissive at a worse corner.
  - **The two settled violations are cross-checked from a second node.**
    `tb_supply_lock.sp` measures the same static offset a second way, as the
    UP/DN pulse-*width* difference at the PFD outputs (`skew_s`) -- different
    nodes, different edges, different `.meas` cards -- and its header states
    that a disagreement between the two is a measurement error rather than a
    design result. At `ff`/27 °C/3.63 V the two agree to 1.76 % and at
    `typical`/−40 °C/3.63 V to 2.26 %. Neither conclusion rests on an
    unverified `phi_b`.
  - **Limitation -- `asymptote_ns` is not a measurement, and no conclusion
    here rests on it.** The table carries a single-pole continuation of the
    two committed samples to t → ∞ at the loop's own τ. Its numerator is a
    difference of two nearly equal numbers, and `20260901-155456-46b92f8`
    section 3c measured this loop's END-plateau residual decaying ≈16× slower
    than single-pole at `ss`/−40 °C, so at an under-damped corner the column
    understates. It is printed to show the **sign and order** of where an
    unsettled row is heading -- "far below `phi_b`" -- and is never used as a
    value. **Where an unsettled corner actually settles is not established by
    this record and is owed to a measurement** (see Result).
  - **Limitation -- nominal skew only.** `sw_stat_global = sw_stat_mismatch =
    0` in the source record. Every number here is the **systematic** static
    offset; the random component is #15's `mc-cp-mismatch` (term 4, |mean| +
    3σ = 0.576 ns) and adds on top of, not instead of, the two settled
    violations below.
  - **Limitation -- one (f_ref, N, trim code) cell.** The whole grid runs at
    f_ref = 12.5 MHz, N = 8, Icp code b1b0 = 10. #394's second scope item asks
    over *which* cells the criterion is reachable; this record answers only
    for the cell this campaign runs. The reference-frequency dependence is
    reasoned about in `spec/pll.md` ("worse at the top of the reference range
    because the charge-derived component scales as ΔQ/Icp while the trim rule
    mandates the smallest Icp") and is not measured anywhere across a
    reference sweep.

  **1. The corner #394 was filed about does not support the claim made from
  it.** `ff`/125 °C/3.63 V: `phi_b` = 1.796 ns, `ferr` = 2.255e-4, so
  `phi_a` = 2.518 ns and the phase fell 0.722 ns -- 72 % of the entire
  criterion -- between the record's own two instants, still falling at the
  second one. That is the largest single-window movement anywhere on the grid.
  A number sampled there is an **upper bound on a decaying tail**; describing
  it as what the loop "stands off in its own undisturbed steady state" is not
  something this evidence can carry. **This does not clear the corner** -- its
  settled value is simply unmeasured, and remains so.

  **2. The 2.7× gap against `pfd-deadzone`'s 0.671 ns is therefore not a gap
  between two measurements of the same thing.** 1.796 ns is a tail sample of a
  closed-loop REF→FB skew at f_ref = 12.5 MHz with the control node standing
  at 2.291 V; 0.671 ns is a settled open-loop charge-null offset at
  f_ref = 25 MHz with the control node pinned at 1.65 V. Three variables
  differ, and the `vctrl_avg_v` column is carried in the table for exactly
  this reason -- it is the join key.

  **Joined on it, the companion record settles which of the three matters.**
  `sim/pfd-deadzone/records/20260916-051356-8cedbba.md` re-measures the same
  open-loop quantity at 12.5 MHz across the whole ratified 0.9-2.4 V control
  window: the reference frequency is worth **−6.4 %** at the cited corner
  (0.671 -> 0.628 ns at 1.65 V), while the control voltage is worth between
  **4x and 87x** at a fixed corner, depending which corner. At `ff`/125 C/3.63 V the closed loop stands at
  2.291 V, where the open-loop systematic term is **0.232 ns** -- so the
  systematic residue explains almost none of the 1.796 ns sampled there, which
  is consistent with, and independent of, finding 1's conclusion that the
  sample is a tail rather than an offset.

  **Where the two campaigns can be compared directly, they agree.** At the
  corners whose closed loop settles nearest a measured open-loop control point:

  | Corner | closed-loop Vctrl | open-loop `t_offset` @ 0.90 V | settled `phi_b` here |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `fs`/-40 C/2.97 V | 0.978 V | 0.897 ns | 0.838 ns |
  | `typical`/-40 C/2.97 V | 0.984 V | 0.923 ns | 0.953 ns |

  Different decks, different topologies (ideal source vs. the real filter),
  different measured nodes, agreeing to within 0.09 ns. That is a genuine
  cross-campaign check on both numbers, and it is the reason this record is
  willing to read the two together at all. It is **not** a general
  reconciliation: it holds at two corners where the operating points happen to
  nearly coincide, and no interpolation of the open-loop curve onto an
  intermediate Vctrl is performed or claimed anywhere in this record.

  **3. Two corners are over the ratified criterion on settled evidence, and
  neither is the one in the spec.** `ff`/27 °C/3.63 V stands off **1.227 ns**
  and `typical`/−40 °C/3.63 V **1.049 ns**, both with the phase essentially
  stationary (11.7 ps and 10.6 ps of movement across a 3.2 µs window, against
  the 0.1 ns settled threshold) and both cross-checked from a second node to
  better than 2.3 %. `ff`/27 °C/3.63 V exceeds the ratified bound by 23 %.
  Both carry `FAIL:lock` in the source record -- the on-chip `lock_detector`
  did not assert at either -- so the flag and the criterion agree at both, and
  `DR-010`'s conclusion that the detector is reporting correctly is unchanged
  and in fact reinforced.

  **4. Every over-bound row that is still moving is moving downward.** All 15
  unsettled over-bound rows have `d_phi_window_ns` < 0. That is a directional
  statement, not a quantitative one: it says the committed `phi_b` at those
  corners over-states the settled offset, and says nothing about where the
  settled offset lands. Two of them (`sf`/−40 °C/3.63 V at 2.136 ns and
  `ff`/125 °C/3.63 V at 1.796 ns) are far enough over the bound that even a
  large decay could leave them outside it.

  **5. Every corner whose own `lock_detector` refused to assert is a corner
  standing off more static phase than the criterion allows — 8 of 8.** The
  source record lists 8 `FAIL:lock` corners and routes them, as a class, to
  "#11, not a loop failure": everything electrical settled and the window
  comparator did not assert. Read against the *ratified* criterion instead of
  the deck's 1.6 ns proxy, **not one of those 8 was inside the bound at the
  instant its phase was sampled.** That is a falsifiable test that did not
  falsify: had the flag been deasserting on parts comfortably within the
  criterion, some of the 8 would have sat well under 1 ns, and none does.

  The strength of the statement differs across the 8, and the difference is
  not glossed here. At 2 of them (`ff`/27 °C/3.63 V, `typical`/−40 °C/3.63 V)
  the phase is settled, so the part is established as outside the criterion
  and the flag is established as correct. At the other 6 the phase was still
  decaying, so the committed value is an upper bound: consistent with the flag
  being correct, not proof of it. **What this does establish is that
  "`FAIL:lock` at these corners is a detector-window question rather than a
  loop question" is not supported by this grid** — at every one of them the
  loop's own measured phase is on the wrong side of the line the flag is
  watching.

  **6. The campaign's acceptance threshold was looser than the spec it was
  read against.** Seven rows carry an overall `PASS` while exceeding the
  ratified 1 ns; only 2 rows were ever flagged `FAIL:phi`, because the deck's
  bound was 1.6 ns at this f_ref. This is why the population above was not
  visible from the record's own verdict column. `run.sh`'s threshold is now
  the ratified criterion itself (see Links); **the source record is not
  re-judged** -- its verdicts stand as committed, and this record's
  `over_deck` column preserves exactly what they were judged at.

  **What is owed.** The settled static phase at the 15 unsettled over-bound

  alone took 11 609 s of ngspice analysis time in the source record, so the
  escalated length at that one corner is of order 10 h. The campaign is named
  in `DR-012` and filed as its own issue rather than attempted here.
