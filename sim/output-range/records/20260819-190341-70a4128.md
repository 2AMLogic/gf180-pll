    The three that matter:
    1. **The campaign row** (reported below), `vctrl_ic` = 1.454 V, run

  - **Why the obvious instrument does not work, stated because it is easy to
    get wrong.** The cheap way to find where a transient stalls is to re-run
    it with shorter `tstop` (this campaign's `SIM_WINCAP`) and see which
    windows finish. That is **invalid for this point**: ngspice derives its
    first transient step from `tstop`, so every distinct `tstop` walks a
    different sequence of timepoints. Short-window re-runs of this point were
    tried first and abort at five different simulated times between 1.78 ns
    and 2.98 ns (`tstop` = 3.0/3.5/3.6/3.7/3.9 ns), each with ngspice's own
    `Timestep too small ... trouble with node "vs5#branch"` -- a divider
    select-line source branch, the same *family* as the `sf`/27 C `lo`
    rows' aborts in the #164 record. (Read from that record's own archived
    logs rather than its summary, those two rows are **not** identical to each
    other: `sf`/27 C/2.97 V aborts on `vs5#branch` at t = 9.47 ns and
    `sf`/27 C/3.30 V on `vs4#branch` at t = 15.93 ns. That record's
    Methodology field describes both with the second one's numbers. It is
    append-only and is not edited for this; the discrepancy is noted here,
    where the logs were re-read, and it does not affect either row's ERROR
    status or any number that record reports.) Suggestive, and it is why
    that hypothesis appears in #168's own Scope, but **none of those runs is
    the trajectory the 2 us run takes**, so none of them is evidence about
    it. They are recorded here as the discarded approach, not as a result;
    one of the five is archived as
    `corners/20260819-190341-70a4128/ff_27c_3.30v_hi_w3.9e-9.log` so that statement rests on
    a committed log rather than on this paragraph.
  - **The instrument actually used.** `stall_trace.sh` leaves `tstop`,
    `tstep`, `tmax`, `.options` and every `.param` alone and instead
    bounds the **number of accepted timepoints** with a `stop after`
    breakpoint before the identical `tran` line, then dumps every vector.
    Bounding the point count rather than the window leaves the trajectory the
    campaign run's own. Confirmed: the 260-timepoint and 2000-timepoint
    traces agree on every timepoint they share, and both reproduce
    t = 1.734625366e-09 s at index 259.
  - **Where the transient stops advancing (Acceptance Criterion 1, part 1).**
    From `corners/20260819-190341-70a4128/trace_ff_27c_3.30v_hi_ic1.454_n2000_timestep.csv`
    (one row per accepted internal timepoint):
    - the run reaches the 100 ps internal ceiling at t = 0.228 ns and holds
      it for 16 consecutive steps;
    - the **last timepoint taken at the ceiling is index 24, t = 1.728000 ns**;
    - by index 27, t = **1.7327852 ns**, every subsequent step is more than
      four orders of magnitude below the ceiling and never recovers;
    - from index 31 onward the internal timestep is a **period-3 limit
      cycle** taking exactly three values -- 3.051758e-15 s, 6.103516e-15 s,
      1.220703e-14 s -- 657/656/656 times respectively over the 1969
      steps from index 31 to 1999.
    - **Steady-state advance: 7.121e-15 s of simulated time per accepted
      timepoint**, i.e. ~1.4e4 timepoints per picosecond. The transient is not
      slow; it is not advancing.
  - **What drives the timestep down (Acceptance Criterion 1, part 2).**
    Ranking every one of the 423 vectors by how far it moves while the
    timestep is collapsed
    (`corners/20260819-190341-70a4128/trace_ff_27c_3.30v_hi_ic1.454_n2000_nodes.csv`) puts
    the same two at the top of both traces, and they are not node voltages:
    **`i(e.xdut.xvco.xcdec1.ec_moscap)` and
    `i(e.xdut.xvco.xcdec2.ec_moscap)`** -- the internal branch variables
    ngspice creates when it expands the two VCO supply decoupling caps
    (`XCDEC1`/`XCDEC2`, `cap_nmos_03v3` 50 u x 50 u across
    `VDD_VCO`/`GND_VCO`), which this PDK models as a *nonlinear*
    `c='...tanh(...)'` expression rather than a fixed value.
    **The ranking alone would be weak evidence and is not what this rests
    on.** Its margin is window-dependent: in the 260-timepoint trace, the
    window immediately after the collapse, these two lead the next-largest
    quantity by ~19x (0.125 vs 6.7e-3); over the longer 2000-timepoint window
    the VCO ring's own outputs have drifted 30-60 mV and the margin narrows
    to ~2x (0.125 vs 6.4e-2 on `v(xdut.xvco.y4)`). The decisive fact is a
    different one -- these two variables are
    **numerically trimodal, in exact lockstep with the timestep**. Over the
    collapsed region each takes exactly **three** distinct values
    (-0.0376885, -0.0188443, +0.0873115; operating-point value 3.3e-12), and
    each value pairs one-to-one with one of the three timestep sizes,
    **657/656/656 times respectively** -- the same counts the timestep CSV
    gives for the three step sizes themselves over index 31..1999, as it must
    be if the pairing is one-to-one:

    | internal timestep | `i(e.xdut.xvco.xcdec1.ec_moscap)` | count |
    |---|---|---|
    | 3.051758e-15 s | -0.0376885 | 657 |
    | 6.103516e-15 s | +0.0873115 | 656 |
    | 1.220703e-14 s | -0.0188443 | 656 |

    Meanwhile `v(vctrl)` is constant to nine decimals (1.454052521 V) for
    the whole 1969-step span. So the solver is cycling between three states
    of the nonlinear-moscap expansion, halving and re-doubling the timestep in
    response, and the circuit is going nowhere. The four loop-filter moscaps
    (`XCF1..4`, `cap_nmos_03v3_b`) show the same three-state signature at
    ~1/34 the amplitude. This is the **same device family, and literally the
    same `XCDEC1` instance path**, that #153 documents ngspice-47
    mis-expanding outright -- see `sim/harness/README.md`'s
    "ngspice-46 required for nested nonlinear moscap decks".
  - **The stall is a solver-trajectory artifact, not a property of this PVT
    corner -- and the evidence that says so also invalidates an earlier
    attempt to blame the moscaps.** Three ablations were run first
    (linearising the two VCO decaps, the four loop-filter caps, and both) and
    all three cleared the stall. Before drawing anything from that, a **null
    control** was run: the same deck, same netlist, nothing ablated, with
    `vctrl_ic` moved from 1.454 V to **1.4540001 V** -- a 1.0e-7 V change,
    which is 1e-3 of the deck's own `vntol` = 1e-4 V absolute voltage
    tolerance and 7e-8 relative against `reltol` = 1e-3. **The control also
    cleared the stall**, and completed the full 2 us window in 173.5 CPU-s --
    inside the 170-420 CPU-s band the other 44 `hi` rows of the grid cost.
    A perturbation the solver's own convergence criteria cannot distinguish
    from zero is therefore sufficient to escape the stall, so the three
    ablations isolate nothing and are **not** offered as evidence here (their
    scratch decks are deliberately not committed, for the same reason -- an
    artifact in `corners/` is a claim, and this one does not survive its own
    control). What
    the control does establish is the answer to question (b): the ff/27 C/
    3.30 V `hi` operating point is ordinary, and the 151-CPU-minute row is
    one hypersensitive trajectory through it, not a corner the circuit cannot
    be simulated at.
  - **Why the perturbed run is reported separately and is NOT the campaign
    row.** It is arguably the same measurement -- the two stimuli differ by
    less than the simulator's own voltage tolerance -- and its
    `vctrl_final` = 1.45653 V sits neatly between its measured supply
    neighbours (`ff`/27 C/2.97 V `hi` = 1.212 V, `ff`/27 C/3.63 V `hi`
    = 1.682 V) with a DN guard PASS, exactly as a well-behaved row of that
    grid should. It is still not the row. The campaign row's `vctrl_ic` is
    produced by `seed_for_corner`'s documented interpolation from #8's
    cited open-loop table; hand-nudging one row's stimulus until it finishes
    is precisely the "make the grid finish" move this repo's conventions
    exist to prevent, and doing it silently would be worse than reporting no
    number. So the grid row below is `CAPPED` and the perturbed run is
    reported beside it, labelled, as a diagnostic.
  - **The recorded outcome for the row (Acceptance Criterion 2), and the
    mechanism that produces it.** `run.sh` gained `SIM_RUNCAP` (#168): a
    per-point **wall-clock budget** for the ngspice invocation, off by
    default. It is deliberately *not* `SIM_WINCAP`: it does not touch
    `tstop`, so a row that completes under it is the same measurement an
    uncapped run would have produced. When the budget fires, `run.sh` kills
    ngspice's whole process group, appends an explicit `SIM_RUNCAP` marker
    line to the log that is then archived verbatim, and reports the row with
    its own status **`CAPPED`** -- never `ERROR`. That distinction is the
    point of the mechanism: `ERROR` on this campaign means the simulator
    reached a verdict and it was a failure (the `sf`/27 C `lo` rows abort
    in ~8 CPU-s naming a node), whereas a budget termination is not a verdict
    at all. The budget is also written into the work-directory tag
    (`..._cap2400`), so a capped log can never be silently reused for an
    uncapped record, and `run.sh`'s record generator tallies `CAPPED`
    separately from `ERROR` and names the budget in the generated text.
    The budget is enforced against a **deadline taken from the clock**, not by
    counting `sleep 1` iterations: the counting form was implemented first
    and measured to overshoot this very budget by 397 s (16.5 %) on this
    contended host, an error proportional to host load. A budget whose real
    value depends on machine load is not a reproducible experimental
    parameter, which would have defeated the purpose. This run's measured
    wall clock was **2402 s** against the 2400 s budget; the remainder
    is the deliberate SIGTERM-to-SIGKILL grace.
    **The budget used here is 2400 s**, chosen to exceed the slowest row that
    has ever *completed* in this grid (`ff`/125 C/3.30 V `lo`, 1818
    CPU-s) by ~32 %, so no row that would have finished can be cut off by it.
  - **What the cap costs in honesty, stated plainly.** A `CAPPED` row
    asserts nothing about whether this loop locks at `ff`/27 C/3.30 V,
    200 MHz. It asserts only that the simulator did not reach a verdict in
    2400 s of wall clock. The grid remains 89 rows with a simulator verdict
    and one without -- but the one without is now reproducible from the
    committed runner with no manual intervention, which the #164 record's own
    Methodology field says its version of this row was not.
  - **Why the row was not simply left to run to completion.** Measured, not
    assumed: at the traced steady-state rate of 7.121e-15 s per timepoint and
    the traced cost of 0.120 CPU-s per timepoint (2000 timepoints in 239.5
    CPU-s), covering the remaining 1.998 us needs ~2.8e8 further timepoints
    and ~3.4e7 CPU-s -- about **390 CPU-days**. Even a hundred-fold
    improvement in the rate leaves the row costing days. This is not a slow
    row that wants a bigger budget; there is no budget that finishes it.
  - **Limitations.**
    - Schematic-level, no parasitics; nominal skew only
      (`sw_stat_global = sw_stat_mismatch = 0`, no Monte Carlo).
    - The root cause is characterised, not fixed. This record says *where*
      the transient stops and *which* variables are cycling with the
      timestep; it does not establish that the nonlinear-moscap expansion is
      the cause, because the null control shows an arbitrarily small stimulus
      change escapes the same stall. A fix would most plausibly live in the
      device model or the simulator, not in this campaign's deck.
    - The trace is 2000 timepoints, not the full stall. The extrapolation
      above assumes the period-3 limit cycle persists; it held over every one
      of the 1969 steps observed, and the #164 attempt's 151 CPU-minutes
      (~7.6e4 timepoints at the traced cost, ~0.5 ns of simulated advance)
      is consistent with it, but it is an extrapolation.
    - Host was contended during the capped run -- two to three other ngspice
      processes, one of them a multi-day run from an unrelated worktree -- as
      this campaign's runner header warns is normal here. That
      affects wall-clock, and therefore how far a wall-clock-budgeted run
      gets before the budget fires -- it does not affect the trace numbers,
      which are per-timepoint.
    - `SIM_RUNCAP` is a wall-clock budget, not a CPU budget, for the same
      reason: it exists to bound how long a human or a pipeline waits.

  **Conclusion.** The `ff`/27 C/3.30 V `hi` point of `sim/output-range`
  does not fail to converge and is not slow -- its transient **stops
  advancing** at t = 1.7328 ns of a 2 us window, after which ngspice's
  internal timestep locks into a period-3 cycle at 3-12 fs and the two VCO
  decoupling moscaps' nonlinear-expansion branch variables cycle between three
  fixed states in exact lockstep with it, while `v(vctrl)` does not move at
  all. Finishing the row from there would cost ~390 CPU-days. The stall is a
  property of that one solver trajectory, not of the operating point: moving
  the control-voltage seed by 1.0e-7 V -- a thousandth of the deck's own
  `vntol` -- makes the identical deck complete the full window in 173.5
  CPU-s. The row is therefore reported **`CAPPED`** under a reproducible
  2400 s per-point wall-clock budget rather than `ERROR` by manual kill,
  and `sim/output-range`'s grid now stands at **89 rows with a simulator
  verdict and one row with a recorded, reproducible non-verdict** -- which is
  a weaker claim than 90/90, stated as such, and a stronger artifact than the
  hand-killed row it replaces.
