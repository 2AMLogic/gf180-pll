  - **Measurement criterion**: frequency is measured on a ring node (`c1`/`e1`/`g1`), not through the output buffer -- the buffer is common to all three counts and would only add a constant, and this deck is a relative comparison.   **startup** (derived) — 0. does the ring oscillate at all, at every point on the grid?

  A current-starved ring must satisfy Barkhausen with the small-signal
  gain its cell actually has: an N-stage single-ended ring needs a
  per-stage gain of 1/cos(pi/N) -- **2.00 for N=3**, 1.24 for N=5, 1.11
  for N=7. A current-starved inverter loses gain as its head and tail
  devices are driven fully on, because their series resistance degenerates
  the stage. So the top of the band is where a low stage count is at risk,
  and that is exactly where this grid finds it.

  it is the *only* viable odd count of the three: each of the other two

      (Its 3.65 pJ per output cycle, 0.69x the 5-stage ring, is not the reason;
      the failures above are disqualifying on their own.)
    - **5 stages -- viable**: clears the 200 MHz v1 ceiling at the slow corner by 28 %, reaches 34 % below the 10 MHz floor at the fast corner, starts at every grid point, keeps full swing (1.01 of supply worst case), and costs 5.31 pJ per output cycle.
