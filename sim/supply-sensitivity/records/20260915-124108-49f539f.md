- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - **What was wrong, precisely.** Two independent defects in one sentence:
    1. **The comparison is inverted for `typical` and inapplicable to `ff`.**
       On the quantity the sentence actually names -- how far a corner's
       *settled* phase sits *below* its window's lower edge -- `ss`/-40 C is
       0.0768 ns below (|0.9232| against 1.0), while `typical`/27 C is
       0.2578 ns below (|-0.9422| against 1.2). `typical` is ~3.4x *further*
       below its own edge, not less far, so "further below ... than either
       other corner" is backwards. And `ff`/125 C is not below its edge at
       all (1.796 ns against 1.2 ns, i.e. *above* it), so it cannot be ranked
       on "distance below the edge" in either direction. (The two
       differences and the 3.4x in this bullet are hand arithmetic on the
       superseded record's own Result table, shown only to demonstrate that
       the sentence's stated comparison is inverted. They are not a claim of
       this record, and nothing below rests on them -- the corrected ranking
       in the Result section rests on the machine-emitted
       `phase_to_shed_ns` column instead.)
    2. **It is the wrong quantity regardless of direction.** A corner's
       settled phase measures how comfortably the detector asserts *once the
       loop has settled*. It says nothing about how far the loop still is
       from settling. The distance from assertion, at the escalated hold's
       own end, is the phase then present (`phi08`) minus the largest phase
       error that still asserts (`asserted_up_to_s`, the window's assert
       edge) -- `phase_to_shed_ns` in the CSV. That is the quantity the
       conclusion needs, and it ranks the corners the same way the conclusion
       does.
  - **Sign convention, stated explicitly** (the second defect #389 names).
    `steady_phase_ns` is a **signed** REF->FB skew: `supply_steady.csv`
    records `-0.9422` ns for `typical`/27 C and `+0.9232` ns for `ss`/-40 C
    at 3.63 V. The detector's window bounds the **magnitude** of the phase
    error (`window_edges.csv` sweeps `terr` magnitude), so every comparison of
    a steady phase against a window edge in this campaign -- including the
    superseded record's own "`typical`: phase 0.9422 ns, inside its 1.2-1.5 ns
    window" -- is a comparison of `|steady_phase_ns|` against the edge. The
    superseded record dropped the sign without saying so; this record states
    the convention rather than restating the number, and the CSV now carries
    the same note as a header comment. **No value changes**: |-0.9422| =
    0.9422, and `typical` is inside its window either way.
  - **`phase_to_shed_ns` is meaningful only where the corner's own steady
    state asserts.** At `ff`/125 C (`steady_lock_ok=no`) the corner is
    structurally outside its detector window even fully settled, so no amount
    of shedding reaches assertion and its 0.3589 ns ranks nothing -- it is
    emitted for completeness and excluded from the ranking, exactly as the
    superseded record's classification already excludes it from the
    settling-tail finding. The ranking below is therefore between the two
    corners classified (3), which is what the conclusion is about.
  - **Inherited limitation -- the detector window is not characterised at
    every voltage this needs.** Unchanged from the superseded record:
    `window_edges.csv` measured `ss`/-40 C at 3.63 V (an exact match to the
    high-plateau rail) but `typical`/27 C at 3.30 V. `typical`'s assert edge
    (1.2 ns), and therefore its 0.6861 ns, carries that mismatch;
    `ss`'s 1.0 ns does not. The window is stated to be an absolute
    inverter-chain delay (`spec/pll.md`, "Lock detector"), which says it
    should not move with the reference period, but neither record measures
    its supply dependence directly. The ranking's ~1.9x margin is large
    relative to the 20 % spread between the two measured assert edges in play
    (1.0 ns and 1.2 ns), but this record does not claim the margin is immune
    to a window shift it has not measured.
  - **`phase_to_shed_ns` is a snapshot, not an extrapolation.** It states how
    far each corner is from assertion at the escalated hold's end (`p8`), not
    when or whether either corner would reach assertion given more time --
    that remains the `sim/loop-dynamics` (#10) question the superseded record
    routed it to. One reading it does support without new arithmetic: the
    superseded record's own decay rates over the same window are -0.29 ns/us
    (`ss`) against -0.24 ns/us (`typical`), so `ss`'s larger remaining
    distance is not an artefact of a slower tail -- it is further away while
    closing faster.
  - **Unchanged from the superseded record, and not re-litigated here**:
    mechanism (1)'s exclusion at all 3 corners, the `ff`/125 C (2) structural
    classification, the `ss`/-40 C and `typical`/27 C (3) settling-tail
    classifications, and every number in that record's Result table. This
    record corrects one comparative; it does not re-open the verdict that
    comparative was decorating.
