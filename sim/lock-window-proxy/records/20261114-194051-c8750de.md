- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - Two decks, one record, one grid (tb.json 'phases'). Phase 'win' measures t_win at all sixteen trim codes in one transient -- sixteen copies of the committed delaywin_3v3 driven from ONE ideal 100 ps step, so every code sees an identical stimulus and the sixteen numbers are simultaneous. Phase 'vco' measures the free-running ring period at two held operating points. Both phases run at every point of the same PVT grid, so each point yields a (T_vco, t_win[0..15]) PAIR rather than two numbers joined across campaigns.
  - DUT is design/netlist/pll_top.spice -- the assembled part's own export, not a block export. The proxy is only meaningful if the ring the tester counts and the delay chain the trim moves come from the same netlist, and pll_top.spice is the only committed export that carries both.
  - The pad-referred observable, stated as a tester would execute it: hold VDD at nominal and the die at 27 C, drive B2:B0 to the band code, force VCTRL to the stated DC level, and count CLK. VCTRL is an iopin of pll_top, B2:B0 are ipins, CLK is an opin -- no new pad, no new test output, nothing internal.
  - Two (band, Vctrl) operating points are measured for the ring, not one: 'a' = band 4 at Vctrl = 1.80 V (mid band, mid control window) and 'b' = band 7 at Vctrl = 2.70 V (fastest band, top of the window). The ring is current-starved, so its delay is not the same blend of nfet and pfet drive at every control voltage; measuring both lets the record report which tracks the delay chain better instead of choosing in advance. If they agree, the ring candidate stands or falls as one thing rather than as an unexplored family of operating points.
  - The proxy RULE under test, stated so it can fail. The trim rule programs the code whose t_win at the reference condition is nearest 1.343 ns in the log sense. The pad-referred substitute assumes only that a part's candidate observable O and its window delay scale together: t_win(c, part) ~= t_win(c, typical) x O(part) / O(typical). Under that assumption the rule becomes 'pick the code whose TYPICAL-bundle window is nearest 1.343 ns x O(typical) / O(part)', which is a fixed sixteen-entry lookup table on one measured number -- exactly the shape of test program a production tester runs. Nothing subtler is assumed, and nothing subtler could be: a tester has one measurement and no knowledge of which corner the part came from.
  - The calibration is ONE bundle, deliberately. t_win(c, typical) and T_vco(typical) are the only fitted quantities, and they are read off the typical bundle; every other bundle is a TEST point, not a fit point. A table fitted to all thirteen bundles and then scored against those same thirteen would be circular and would prove nothing.
  - Acceptance is sufficiency, not correlation. The residual between the proxy's prediction and the measured window is reported, but the verdict is the band: for each bundle, the code the proxy selects is evaluated over that bundle's own nine voltage/temperature points against the ratified [1, 2] ns band -- the same test sim/lock-window-trim applies to the code the true rule selects. A proxy that picks a different code than the rule but still holds the band at every point of every bundle has executed the rule's INTENT, which is what the spec's Lock detector row is conditioned on.
  - The window measurement is cross-checked against the committed single-copy campaign rather than assumed equivalent. sim/lock-window-trim/records/20260917-185928-8adff3d.md measured the same cell one code at a time at tran 5p 40n; this deck measures sixteen copies at once at the same settings. The 'twin_crosscheck' table reports the point-by-point ratio between the two, so a loading or stimulus artefact from putting sixteen copies on one source would show up as a systematic offset instead of hiding.
  - The six passive-only bundles are NOT a formality here, and the grid is run at the full thirteen for that reason. Every device in delaywin_3v3 is an nfet_03v3/pfet_03v3, so the WINDOW is degenerate with typical across res_*/moscap_*/mimcap_* by construction -- sim/lock-window-trim measured exactly that null. The RING is not: design/netlist/vco.spice biases the starved ring through three ppolyf_u_3k poly resistors (XRCG, XROFF, XRDEG -- DR-009's device class), so a resistor skew moves the ring period while leaving the window untouched. A pad-referred candidate built on the ring therefore has a failure mode that no MOS-only sweep can see, and the res_ff/res_ss bundles are the points that expose it. The CLK -> FB and CLK -> DIVOUT skews inherit a weaker version of the same sensitivity through the CLK edge rate they are triggered on.
  - Limitation -- thirteen bundles are not a process distribution. A corner grid can show that a candidate breaks at a skew, and can bound its residual over the skews it contains; it cannot stand in for a Monte Carlo over the real global-variation distribution, and this record does not claim it does. all-slow and all-fast are not redundant with ss and ff for the same reason the paragraph above gives -- they differ from them in the passive axes the ring responds to.
  - Limitation -- on-die skews, not pad-to-pad skews. The two skew candidates are measured between nodes of design/netlist/pll_top.spice. On silicon a tester triggers on the CLK pad and times the FB or DIVOUT pad, so the measured number additionally carries the difference of two output-path delays -- and design/ contains no output pad driver for FB, DIVOUT or LOCK at all (CLK alone is buffered, by the VCO's own three-stage tapered buffer). Those drivers are the harness's, not this block's, so their PVT behaviour is outside what any record here can bound. A candidate that fails on the on-die skew fails a fortiori at the pads; a candidate that passed would still owe that term.
  - Unaffected by #515. That issue reports pll_top leaving the lock detector's LDT3 input on an auto-named net, so only 8 of the 16 codes are reachable through the assembled part's pins. This deck's window phase instantiates delaywin_3v3 directly and drives all four trim pins from its own sources, so all sixteen codes are measured on the committed cell regardless. The DUT is pll_top.spice because the ring and the delay chain have to come from one netlist; nothing here reads the trim through pll_top's own wiring of it.
  - Limitation -- schematic-level. No extracted parasitics, on either side of the mapping. The delay chain's trim segments are of order 1 fF and the ring's stages are small; extraction (#18) moves both, and there is no reason it moves them by the same factor. The proxy's residual must be re-taken post-extraction before the procedure is treated as final.
  - Limitation -- no mismatch. sw_stat_global = sw_stat_mismatch = 0. The proxy compares a five-stage ring against a four-stage delay chain on different parts of the die; die-to-die that is the global term this campaign sweeps, but within-die mismatch between the two is exactly what a corner grid cannot see.
  - Simulator settings: phase 'win' at tran 5p 40n, matching sim/lock-window-trim's deck so the cross-check is a comparison of results and not of settings; phase 'vco' at tran 20p 700n with a 200 ps maximum timestep, the period taken as one eighth of the span between the 1st and the 9th rising edge after ktsettle = 150 ns, so neither the ring's seeded start-up nor the divider's power-up transient is folded into a number, and the eight-period span averages away the per-edge timestep quantisation the 20 ps step would otherwise leave on a 2.6 ns band-7 period.
