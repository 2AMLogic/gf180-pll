- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - **Timing-mismatch measurement**: two charge-pump instances are pulsed from the SAME control edge, one hardwired UP-active and one DN-active, each into its own control node held at `vctrl` by an ideal source. Sharing the stimulus edge is the point: any measured UP-vs-DN difference is then the pump's own asymmetry, with no stimulus skew mixed in. Per polarity the deck reports `i_ss` (steady state late in the 5 ns pulse), `q` (charge over the whole switching event, INCLUDING the tail-node charge-sharing transient at switch-on) and `w_eff = q / i_ss`, the effective pulse width the loop actually sees. `wskew = w_up - w_dn` is the headline number.
  - **The measurements are taken in the control block, not as netlist `.measure` cards, and that is load-bearing.** The 50 % turn-on/turn-off delays trigger on a threshold of `i_ss/2` -- half a value that only exists once the transient has run. A netlist-level `.measure ... val='...'` expression is expanded by ngspice's `.param` machinery before the simulation starts and cannot reference a measurement result, so the pre-migration deck computed `iup_h`/`idn_h` with `let` after the `meas` that produced `iup_ss`. This manifest keeps exactly that sequence by putting it in `analyses` (which the harness passes through to `.control` verbatim) and exporting each result through the manifest's `measure` block. `td=` is likewise load-bearing, and is needed on the TARG clause as well as the TRIG clause: ngspice searches each clause independently from t=0 unless told otherwise, so without it the turn-OFF measurements latch onto a crossing belonging to the turn-ON event and report a negative delay.
  - **One ngspice invocation per (PVT corner, control voltage)** -- 45 x 3 = 135 points, the pre-migration grid unchanged. `vctrl` is a declared `sweeps` axis rather than a run.sh loop variable, taking the two ends and the middle of DR-001 Decision 2's ~0.9-2.4 V window.
  - The trim code is held at the nominal b1 b0 = 10 (3 unit legs). The trim RANGE is characterized by the DC bench; the switching asymmetry is a property of the shared steering switch and the tail node, which the unit-element trim construction leaves unchanged.
  - **Bias generation is out of scope and idealized**: the four bias nodes are driven from ideal current sources at 4x the unit-leg current, exactly as in the DC bench and in `sim/devchar-cp`. The skew reported here is therefore the OUTPUT STAGE's own.
  - Transient: 20 ns at a 5 ps maximum timestep, `reltol` 1e-4 / `abstol` 1e-14. `rshunt = 1e12` resolves the disabled trim legs' floating cascode mid-node (3.3 pA at 3.3 V, below the abstol floor). `itl4 = 200` raises the transient Newton-iteration limit -- a solver-EFFORT knob, not a tolerance: every convergence tolerance is unchanged, so the converged solution is unchanged (verified bit-for-bit at typical/27C/3.30V/Vctrl=1.65V, which converges either way). It is needed because the DR-005 dump-node buffer puts two MOS gates on the control node, and the ideal source holding that node has to carry their displacement current: with everything else in DC steady state that branch current numerically jitters at a few times the 1e-14 A abstol, and ngspice's default 10 iterations are not enough to settle it, so it halves the timestep to nothing at 2 of the 135 points (both at Vctrl = 2.4 V on the 3.63 V supply). Raising abstol instead does NOT fix it and would have loosened a real tolerance.
  - **Limitation (nominal skew only)**: `sw_stat_global = sw_stat_mismatch = 0`. Everything here is systematic; RANDOM device mismatch is #15's campaign (`mc-cp-mismatch`), and these numbers are what that distribution should be centred on.
