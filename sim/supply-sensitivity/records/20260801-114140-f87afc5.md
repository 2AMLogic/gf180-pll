- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - **Reproducible, not asserted.** The classification is produced by
    `sim/supply-sensitivity/testbench/reexamine.sh <record-id>`, committed

    `corners/20260801-114140-f87afc5/reexamination.csv` (SHA-256

    reproduces it exactly.
  - **The signature being tested for**, quoted from `sim/README.md`'s
    "Closed-loop internal-timestep bound" (which takes it from #52's
    `sim/pll-top-smoke/records/20260801-085349-0e5c22d.md`): each miss "is a
    feedback edge the PFD never sees, so the loop reads as **jammed with UP
    asserted, ramping Vctrl to the rail while the feedback is ALREADY faster
    than the reference**".

    Both halves of that sentence are measurable from the committed CSV, and
    the test is that they must hold **together** -- either alone is ordinary:
    1. **Is the feedback already faster than the reference?**
       `f_fb = f_out / nmeas`, using the MEASURED divide ratio so the test
       does not assume the divider worked, against `f_ref` = 12.5 MHz.
       Threshold: the campaign's own `ACC_FERR` = 1e-3. Not a new number.
    2. **Is Vctrl being driven the WRONG way?** Against #8's independent
       open-loop prediction of the control voltage that produces 100 MHz at
       that corner. A loop whose Vctrl sits far **above** the value that would
       give the target frequency, while its feedback is already too **fast**,
       is being driven away from lock -- which negative feedback with a
       working detector cannot do. Threshold: 0.10 V.
    3. **Had the run settled at all?** Late-window Vctrl spread
       (`vctrl_max - vctrl_min`). Threshold: 0.05 V.

    **Thresholds 2 and 3 are calibrated against the compliant rows in the same
    record, not chosen to produce a result.** The four rows that PASSED agree
    with #8's independent prediction to within **4-12 mV** and have a
    late-window spread of **5-7 mV**. The 0.10 V and 0.05 V thresholds are
    ~8-20x and ~7x outside those bands respectively. Every row classified
    below falls decisively on one side or the other -- the smallest "excess"
    among the diverging rows is 188 mV and the largest among the settled rows
    is 14 mV, a separation of more than 13x with nothing in between. **No row
    sits near a threshold**, so the conclusion does not depend on where in
    those gaps the thresholds were placed.
  - **The two forms the artefact takes here**, distinguished because they are
    different failures with the same cause:
    - `ARTEFACT-DIVERGING` -- feedback already fast, Vctrl driven above the
      predicted operating point, still moving at the end of the window.
    - `ARTEFACT-DETECTOR` -- the loop is **settled at the correct
      frequency and the correct control voltage**, and the design's own LOCK
      flag still never asserted. `lock_detector` is driven from UP/DN
      (`design/README.md`: `ERR = XOR(UP,DN)`), so an integrator that steps
      over the PFD's pulses cannot produce LOCK even for a loop that is
      locked. This is the cleanest single instance in the record, because
      there is no competing explanation: nothing about a genuinely
      unlocked loop puts it on frequency with 9 mV of control-voltage motion.
  - **What this record does NOT establish.** It does not produce corrected
    values for the five affected rows -- it cannot, without simulation. It
    establishes only that those five rows' verdicts are **not evidence about
    this design** and must not be cited as such. In particular it does **not**
    claim the loop would pass at those corners: a re-run at the bound could
    still fail them, for reasons this analysis cannot see.

  detector pulses. That asymmetry is what lets this record qualify five rows
  without invalidating the whole grid.
