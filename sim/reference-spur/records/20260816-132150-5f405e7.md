- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - MEASUREMENT, NOT DERIVATION. spec/pll.md's Reference spur row was a target backed by a DERIVATION -- sim/cp-compliance's charge-pump per-event charge asymmetry plus sim/mc-cp-mismatch's statistical residual, pushed through a narrowband-FM approximation to -61 dBc -- with no closed-loop spectral measurement anywhere in sim/, and the Verification owed table said so. This record is that measurement: the assembled pll_top runs to lock and derive.py reads the sideband at f_out - f_ref and f_out + f_ref straight out of the output spectrum. The derivation is a cross-check here, not a target to reproduce.
  - WHY A TRANSIENT AND NOT AN AC ANALYSIS. The spur is a nonlinear, periodic-steady-state effect: once per reference cycle the PFD fires both branches for the reset-delay overlap, the charge pump's per-event charge asymmetry lands on C2, and the resulting Vctrl ripple phase-modulates the ring. sim/loop-dynamics' small-signal loop analysis cannot produce it and says so in its own Limitations field. A transient to lock plus a spectral reduction is the only method on this flow that can.
  - OPERATING POINT: f_ref = 25 MHz, N = 6, f_out = 150 MHz, VCO band code 6, Icp trim code 0 (one unit leg). f_ref = 25 MHz is the top of the ratified 1-25 MHz reference range and one unit leg is the trim the Icp trim-code rule REQUIRES there (spec/pll.md, from DR-006 Decision 5) -- not a discretionary choice. N = 6 is inside the ratified 4-64 range and f_out = 150 MHz inside the ratified 10-200 MHz output band. Band 6 is the lowest band code that reaches 150 MHz at the nominal corner (band 5 tops out at 124.5 MHz there), which is the band-selection rule spec/pll.md makes normative; band 5 does reach 150 MHz at the hottest/slowest corners, but the band code is a STATIC configuration input (DR-001 Decision 2) and cannot be re-chosen per corner, so 6 is the correct single setting. Every configuration bit is generated by sim/lib/pll_top_dut.sh and re-verified against it by check_config.sh in this directory.
  - WHY 150 MHz AND NOT 200 MHz -- and how to read the number against the spec's binding point. The spur scales with f_out (theta = 2*pi*f_out*TIE), so the ratified band's 200 MHz ceiling is the binding frequency and spec/pll.md's derivation is stated there. 200 MHz is not reachable in one band code across this PVT grid: band 6's ceiling falls to 166.3 MHz at the slowest corner while band 7's floor rises to 225.4 MHz at the fastest, so no single static band code holds 200 MHz over the corners (sim/vco-tuning-range record 20260804-162735-72883fb). 150 MHz is the highest output frequency one static band code holds at every corner of the mandated matrix. Scaling a measured spur from 150 MHz to 200 MHz is +20*log10(200/150) = +2.50 dB, reported as its own column (spur_dbc_at_200mhz) -- arithmetic on the narrowband-FM relation, not a measurement, and labelled as such.
  - LOCK CRITERION (the same one sim/pll-top-smoke uses). Locked when (a) the REF->FB delay measured at two late instants ta and tb does not drift -- in a type-II loop a residual frequency error slips that delay at exactly (df/f) seconds per second, so differencing it gives the residual fractional frequency error `ferr` directly, to picosecond resolution -- and (b) that static phase error is small against a reference period. ta and tb are placed on a reference half-period so the REF and FB edges picked are the same cycle's pair and cannot alias by a whole reference period. `nmeas` = f_out/f_fb is measured independently, so a divider running at the wrong N shows up as a ratio rather than hiding inside a single frequency.
  - STEADY STATE: WHAT IS ASSUMED, WHAT IS MEASURED. The loop's slow closed-loop pole is 1/(R*C1) = 9.3 us regardless of Icp (spec/pll.md's ~43 us structural settling floor, DR-006 Decision 7), so a residual control-voltage error decays one e-fold per 9.3 us and no practical run length drives it to zero. Two things follow, and both are done rather than assumed. FIRST, the loop is released at the corner's own predicted lock point rather than at a common voltage: `vstart` is the control voltage at which sim/vco-tuning-range's COMMITTED f(Vctrl) table (record 20260804-162735-72883fb, band 6) says that corner's ring runs at 150 MHz, interpolated by vstart_from_vco_record.py in this directory, which also re-checks the manifest against that record. Across this grid the lock point spans 1.09-2.43 V, so releasing every corner at one voltage would spend tens of microseconds just slewing the control node at Icp/C1 = 14 mV/us before any measurement could start. SECOND, the residual that remains is MEASURED, not assumed away: derive.py fits the control-node slope in every spectral window and converts it to `drift_q_fc`, the extra charge that drift moves per reference cycle, to be read against the 2.16 fC median / 3.68 fC worst-corner charge-pump asymmetry the spur is made of (sim/cp-compliance record 20260731-194124-afa338c via DR-006 section 8). A point whose drift charge is comparable to that asymmetry is reporting an upper bound on the spur, and the record says so.
  - TWO NUMBERS, DELIBERATELY. `spur_dbc` is the direct measurement in the LAST spectral window and is what the checks score. It is conservative: residual settling drift adds charge to the same node the asymmetry lands on, so it can only make the measured spur worse, never better. `spur_dbc_zero_drift_fit` is the same measurement extrapolated to zero drift -- the sideband amplitude is linear in the net per-cycle charge, so a straight line through the run's (drift charge, sideband amplitude) pairs, evaluated at zero, is the settled value, with `fit_r2` reporting how well that linear model held. `spur_dbc_first_window` is the first window's value, so a reader can see the number converging across the run rather than taking convergence on trust.
  - SPECTRAL REDUCTION (derive.py, stdlib only -- sim/harness is stdlib-only by design and a handful of DFT bins does not justify adding numpy to this repo's simulation flow). The deck writes v(clk) and v(vctrl) with `wrdata` over the measurement region only: ngspice's .tran tstart argument stores nothing before it, so the acquisition costs no data and no memory. The reduction resamples the non-uniformly-sampled waveform onto a uniform grid of 512 samples per reference period with a local four-point Lagrange CUBIC (a linear interpolant leaves a broadband reconstruction floor near -74 dBc against the ~300 ps edges this output has, only ~13 dB below the level being reported and enough to bias a -61 dBc sideband by ~2 dB; the cubic drops that floor to ~-84 dBc -- both numbers measured, and pinned, in sim/tests/test_reference_spur_derive.py), applies a periodic Hann window over EXACTLY 16 reference periods, and evaluates the DFT at exactly k*f_ref. In lock the whole loop is periodic at f_ref, so every line of the spectrum -- carrier at 6*f_ref, sidebands at 5*f_ref and 7*f_ref -- lands exactly on a bin: coherent, no scalloping loss, no peak search that could pick the wrong bin. The Hann window is deliberate: a rectangular window is leakage-free only for a perfectly stationary carrier, and a still-settling loop moves the carrier by a fraction of a bin, whose rectangular-window leakage falls only as 1/(bin distance) -- Hann's falls as 1/(distance^3), which puts carrier leakage into the sideband bin far below the -60 dBc being looked for. The reported spur is the LARGER (worse) of the two sidebands.
  - THE REDUCTION IS TESTED AGAINST KNOWN ANSWERS, not only against itself. sim/tests/test_reference_spur_derive.py (harness unit-test suite, no PDK, no ngspice) synthesises a phase-modulated square wave with a chosen peak phase deviation on a deliberately NON-uniform time grid -- the one property of ngspice's output that makes this DFT non-trivial -- and asserts that both the DFT path and the independent TIE path return the 20*log10(theta/2) dBc the waveform was built with, at -45, -55, -61 and -65 dBc, to within 1 dB. It also pins the reduction's own spectral floor on an unmodulated carrier, the zero-drift extrapolation's arithmetic, and the window list this manifest's wa/wb/wcyc describe. The failure mode being guarded against is specific: a windowing, resampling or normalisation mistake here produces a plausible-looking spur rather than an obvious error.
  - TIE CROSS-CHECK. The same waveform is reduced a second, independent way: every rising mid-supply crossing of v(clk) in the window, de-trended by its own best-fit ramp into a TIE sequence, whose f_ref component gives a peak phase deviation theta and a narrowband-FM spur 20*log10(theta/2). It shares no arithmetic with the DFT path -- it reads only edge crossing times, where the waveform is steepest and interpolation error is smallest, while the DFT integrates the whole waveform including the flat tops. `spur_tie_dbc` agreeing with `spur_dbc` is the evidence that neither the resampling grid nor the window is manufacturing the answer. Disagreement would be data too: it would mean the sideband carries a non-phase (amplitude or waveform-shape) component once per reference cycle, which the DFT sees and the TIE path does not.
  - SIMULATOR SETTINGS. .tran 2n 8u 2.4u 100p -- the fourth argument is the 100 ps ceiling on the ngspice INTERNAL timestep that every campaign whose DUT contains the PFD inherits (sim/README.md, 'Closed-loop internal-timestep bound'; SIMENV_CLOSED_LOOP_TMAX in sim/lib/simenv.sh). It is a property of the PFD's internal 0.33-0.39 ns SR-latch set pulse, not of this testbench, and violating it produces a confident false 'the loop does not lock'. A tb.json manifest does NOT inherit that shell constant -- so it is written here as an explicit .tran argument and then VERIFIED per point: `dt_int_mean` is the mean internal timestep derived from the waveform the deck actually wrote, checked against a 100 ps ceiling, so a run whose bound silently defaulted to the print step fails the record instead of quietly biasing it. .option rshunt=1e12 itl4=200 reltol=1e-3 abstol=1e-13 vntol=1e-6 -- identical to sim/pll-top-smoke's, so this deck's converged solution is comparable with the other closed-loop campaigns'. The standing DN-branch guard (sim/README.md) is carried as the `dn_lvl` check.
  - CORNER MATRIX -- five points, one per grid block, chosen so that the union spans every mandated axis: all five MOS bundles, all three temperatures (-40/27/125 C) and all three supplies (2.97/3.30/3.63 V). It is NOT the 45-point cross-product, and the reason is stated plainly rather than implied: one point here is a closed-loop transient of the whole PLL at a 100 ps internal-timestep ceiling -- ~70 000 internal steps per microsecond of simulated time, thousands of CPU-seconds per point -- and the full cross-product at this run length is several CPU-days. What makes the subset defensible rather than merely cheap is that the spur's PVT dependence enters through two quantities that ARE characterized over the full matrix elsewhere: the charge-pump per-event charge asymmetry (sim/cp-compliance, 45 points) and the VCO gain (sim/vco-tuning-range, 63 points). What no other record has, and what this one supplies, is the measured closed-loop transfer from that charge to an actual spectral sideband. The full-grid re-run remains owed and spec/pll.md's Verification owed table says so.
  - LIMITATIONS. (1) MOS-only sweep: res_typical / moscap_typical / mimcap_typical are held at typical at every point, so the loop filter's R, C1 and C2 do not skew. That matters more here than for most campaigns -- the peak ripple is dQ/C2 and C2 is a MIM cap -- so this record bounds the spur against MOS skew only; the C2 spread is covered by DR-006 Decision 1's worst-case minimum, which the spec's own derivation already uses. (2) One (f_ref, N, band, trim) operating point of the ratified space, at 150 MHz rather than the binding 200 MHz; see the scaling note above. (3) Schematic-level: no layout parasitics, and no supply or substrate coupling of f_ref into the VCO rail -- spec/pll.md budgets several dB of the -55 dBc target for exactly those mechanisms and this record measures none of them. (4) The reference is ideal: no reference jitter, no duty-cycle error. (5) Device mismatch is OFF (no Monte Carlo): this is the systematic, corner-driven spur, and the statistical mismatch contribution is sim/mc-cp-mismatch's (#15), additive to it in the spec's derivation. (6) Not a lock-time result: the control node is deliberately released near its lock point, so no acquisition-time number may be read from this record -- sim/lock-time (#12) owns that claim.
