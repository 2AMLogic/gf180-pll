# Record 20260925-090649-4422f1d

- **Record ID**: 20260925-090649-4422f1d
- **Claim**: #511 -- DR-012 Decision 4b states that where a given
  `(f_out, band code, N, trim code)` cell lands on the control window "is set
  by the [band-selection rule](../../../spec/pll.md#band-selection-rule),
  **which this record does not re-derive**", having just established that the
  control voltage — not the PVT corner — is the axis the ratified `<= 1 ns`
  Lock criterion is lost on. `spec/pll.md`'s
  [Verification owed](../../../spec/pll.md#verification-owed) row for the Lock
  criterion carries the consequence: what is owed includes "(b) any
  **closed-loop** measurement at an `(f_ref, N, trim-code)` cell other than
  12.5 MHz / N = 8 / b1b0 = 10 … so a closed-loop cell that parks low on that
  window is expected to miss and has never been run" and "(c) the design
  resolution, which DR-012 Decision 7 deliberately does not pick".

  **This record does the re-derivation Decision 4b named and left undone**, and
  it changes what is owed on both counts. It re-simulates nothing and re-judges
  nothing: every number is arithmetic on three committed grids.

  **Answer, in three parts.**

  1. **The worst of the two settled violations is at a band the normative rule
     does not select.** At `ff`/27 °C the committed grid runs **band 6**, with
     the control node at 1.006–1.392 V; the rule — "the lowest 3-bit band code
     that reaches `f`" — selects **band 5** over DR-003 Decision 5's measured
     0.9–2.7 V window, which puts the same corner at **1.967–2.595 V**, a full
     0.96–1.20 V higher. That is 1 of the 15 (bundle, temperature) cells; the
     other 14 agree. It is also the cell carrying **1.227 ns**, the largest
     settled violation on the grid and the number DR-012 Decision 3 leads with.

  2. **`typical`/−40 °C/3.63 V is at the rule-selected band under every
     reading, and its 1.049 ns stands.** So the design gap does not evaporate:
     it goes from two settled violations to **one at a rule-compliant
     configuration, plus one at a configuration no compliant part carries and
     which has never been measured**.

  3. **Applying the rule is not a fix.** Swept across the ratified 10–200 MHz
     output band, the rule still parks at least one corner at `Vctrl <= 1.05 V`
     at **23 of the 39** sampled output frequencies, down to **0.919 V**
     (`ss`/125 °C/2.97 V, band 6, f_out = 135 MHz). The measured systematic
     term alone stays inside the criterion almost everywhere it does so — 2 of
     1628 rule-selected points exceed 1 ns — but on DR-012 Decision 4a's own
     summed budget (systematic + 0.576 ns statistical + 0.239 ns retiming) the
     criterion is missed at **1397 of 1628 (85.8 %)**. The band-selection axis
     therefore **narrows the gap and cannot close it**, which is the sentence
     DR-012 Decision 7 needed to choose between its four candidate loci.

  The decision record this backs is
  `spec/decision-records/DR-024-band-selection-is-not-the-lock-criterion-resolution.md`.

  **The two selectors, stated precisely, because the finding is their
  difference.**

  | Selector | Definition | Where it comes from |
  |---|---|---|
  | **rule** | the *lowest* band code whose measured `f(Vctrl)` curve reaches the target at **all three** supplies with `Vctrl` inside a stated window | `spec/pll.md` [Band-selection rule](../../../spec/pll.md#band-selection-rule), normative. "All three supplies" is this record's reading of it, forced by DR-001 Decision 2: band select is a static input with no calibration FSM, so one code must hold across the ratified rail |
  | **campaign** | the band whose worst-case `abs(Vctrl − mid)` over the three supplies is smallest, `mid` being the midpoint of a **0.85–2.70 V** search window | `run.sh`'s `derive_op_points`, transcribed. It is the only band selector in `sim/` that is not the rule — `sim/pll-top-smoke` states and applies the rule by name |

  **The window is an argument, not a constant, and that is load-bearing.**
  DR-001 Decision 2 *predicted* a 0.9–2.4 V usable control window; DR-003
  Decision 5 *measured* 0.9–2.7 V on 504 curves, and `spec/pll.md`
  ([Interfaces](../../../spec/pll.md#interfaces), "Vctrl operating window
  0.9 – 2.7 V") carries the measured one as operative. `spec/pll.md`'s
  band-selection rule states "reaches `f`" **without naming a window**, and at
  `ff`/27 °C / 100 MHz the two readings select different bands — so the record
  reports the rule under both rather than picking one silently.

  **Limitations, stated rather than buried.**

  - **The systematic term at the rule-selected `ff`/27 °C/3.63 V cell is
    UNMEASURED.** The rule parks it at 2.595 V; `sim/pfd-deadzone`'s grid tops
    out at 2.40 V, which is DR-001 Decision 2's predicted window top. The
    script refuses to extrapolate there and writes `unmeasured` — 121 of the
    section-2 points are in the same condition. What the measured grid *does*
    say at that corner is the trend the cell sits on the far side of:
    1.8229 ns at 0.90 V, 0.6570 at 1.65, **0.1750 at 2.40**.
  - **Every `t_sys` here is open-loop, at f_ref = 12.5 MHz.** A cell at another
    `f_out` runs at another `f_ref` under the [Icp trim-code
    rule](../../../spec/pll.md#icp-trim-code-rule). DR-012 measured that axis
    as nearly irrelevant (−6.4 % over a 2:1 change in f_ref at the cited
    corner), so it is carried as the operating-point term it is and **not** as
    a closed-loop prediction. Section 2 is a map of where the rule puts the
    control node; it is not a substitute for running the loop there.
  - **The interpolation is on three measured control voltages** (0.90 / 1.65 /
    2.40 V). The systematic term is strongly non-linear in `Vctrl` — that
    non-linearity is DR-012's own headline — so an interpolated value between
    grid points carries the error of a piecewise-linear read of a convex curve.
    Where an interpolated number is load-bearing below it is stated as
    interpolated, and the closed-loop measurement it is compared against is
    quoted beside it.
  - **DR-012 Decision 4a's budget sum is a worst-case linear sum**, and it is
    used here exactly as that decision states it rather than re-derived. It is
    conservative against the nominal-skew closed-loop grid, whose settled
    phases span 0.14–1.23 ns. The 85.8 % figure is therefore a statement about
    the **budget**, not a prediction of 1397 failing measurements.
  - **No `mc-cp-mismatch` run, no post-extraction data, no new corner.** This
    record moves no verdict in any committed record.

- **Result**:

  ### 1. The committed 100 MHz grid, selector against selector

  All 45 rows are in `corners/20260925-090649-4422f1d/band_rule_100mhz.csv`.
  The two selectors agree at **14 of the 15** (bundle, temperature) cells. The
  one they do not agree at is `ff`/27 °C:

  | Supply | campaign band 6 `Vctrl` | measured `t_sys` there | rule band 5 `Vctrl` | measured `t_sys` there | closed-loop `phi_b` (band 6) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2.97 V | 1.008 V | 0.9121 ns | 1.967 V | **0.3333 ns** | −0.048 ns (unsettled) |
  | 3.30 V | 1.215 V | 0.9490 ns | 2.295 V | **0.3548 ns** | −0.020 ns (unsettled) |
  | 3.63 V | 1.394 V | **1.0546 ns** | 2.595 V | *unmeasured* (0.1750 ns at 2.40 V) | **1.227 ns, settled, `FAIL:lock`** |

  `t_sys` columns are `sim/pfd-deadzone`'s measured open-loop systematic term
  interpolated at that control voltage. The band choice moves it by **2.7×** at
  2.97 V and **2.7×** at 3.30 V; at 3.63 V the rule-selected cell is off the
  measured grid, on the far side of a corner whose measured term falls from
  1.8229 ns to 0.1750 ns across the window.

  The campaign's own `vctrl_avg_v` (1.006 / 1.21 / 1.392 V) reproduces the
  band-6 derivation to within 2 mV at all three supplies, which is what
  licenses reading the two grids together at all.

  **The other settled violation is unaffected.** At `typical`/−40 °C both
  selectors choose band 6 under both window readings, putting the node at
  1.476 V (the loop settled at 1.471 V) where the measured systematic term is
  0.8354 ns and the closed loop settles at **1.049 ns** — over the ratified
  bound, settled, at a rule-compliant configuration.

  **A third reading, reported because it constrains the rule's own
  statement.** Under DR-001 Decision 2's *predicted* 0.9–2.4 V window the rule
  has **no answer at all** at 4 of the 15 cells at 100 MHz — `typical`/27 °C,
  `ss`/−40 °C, `fs`/27 °C and `sf`/27 °C, where no single static band code
  reaches 100 MHz across the ±10 % rail without leaving that window. Those are
  the same 4 points `20260925-044237-4ff4f65` graded as leaving the predicted
  window and not the measured one. The rule is satisfiable at all 15 cells only
  under DR-003 Decision 5's measured 0.9–2.7 V window, which is the reading
  used everywhere else in this record.

  ### 2. Can the rule keep the loop off the bottom of the window?

  The rule is evaluated at 39 output frequencies across the ratified
  10–200 MHz band × 45 PVT points. Full table:
  `corners/20260925-090649-4422f1d/band_rule_output_band.csv`.

  | Question | Answer |
  |---|---|
  | rule-selected points with a measured `t_sys` | 1628 (+121 parked above the offset grid's 2.40 V top, +2 (f_out, cell) with no feasible static band) |
  | `t_sys` **alone** over the ratified 1 ns | **2 of 1628 (0.1 %)** |
  | DR-012 4a budget sum over the ratified 1 ns | **1397 of 1628 (85.8 %)** |
  | output frequencies parking some corner at `Vctrl <= 1.05 V` | **23 of 39** |
  | lowest rule-selected `Vctrl` anywhere | **0.919 V**, `ss`/125 °C/2.97 V, band 6, f_out = 135 MHz (`t_sys` 0.8762 ns, budget sum 1.6912 ns) |
  | worst rule-selected `t_sys` | 1.0052 ns, `ff`/125 °C/3.63 V, band 4, f_out = 40 MHz, `Vctrl` 1.423 V |

  Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions, which is why both
  are stated.

  **The rule bounds the systematic half well.** Left to the rule, the measured
  systematic term stays inside the whole ratified criterion at 1626 of 1628
  points, mean 0.3954 ns. It does so partly for a reason the rule was not
  designed for: the bottom-of-window cells it does produce land at the
  **2.97 V** supply, where the measured term at 0.90 V is 0.90–1.05 ns, while
  the 3.63 V supply — where that same term reaches 1.72–1.87 ns — always needs
  a *higher* control voltage to reach the same frequency and so never parks at
  the bottom. That coupling is measured, not designed, and nothing in the
  specification protects it.

  **It cannot buy back the budget.** Meeting the ratified 1 ns on DR-012 4a's
  summation needs `t_sys <= 0.185 ns`; the rule-selected mean is 0.3954 ns and
  the rule-selected floor of the control window is 0.919 V, where the term is
  0.8762 ns — 88 % of the entire criterion with nothing left for the other two
  terms. **No band-selection policy on this band map keeps the loop out of the
  region DR-012 Decision 4b measured the criterion is lost in**, because the
  band boundaries are fixed and the VCO's PVT spread is wider than one code's
  overlap: at every output frequency some corner has to sit near the bottom of
  *some* band.

  ### 3. What this hands to the owed measurement

  `spec/pll.md`'s Verification-owed item (b) asks for a closed-loop cell that
  parks low on the control window, at an `(f_ref, N, trim-code)` cell other
  than 12.5 MHz / N = 8 / b1b0 = 10. Section 2 names it rather than leaving it
  to be chosen: **f_out = 135 MHz at `ss`/125 °C**, band 6 per the rule, which
  parks at **0.919 / 1.201 / 1.463 V** across the rail — the lowest
  rule-compliant parking anywhere in the ratified output band. At N = 8 that is
  f_ref = 16.875 MHz, which the [Icp trim-code
  rule](../../../spec/pll.md#icp-trim-code-rule) maps to the 16 MHz row (one
  unit leg, b1b0 = 00) — a different cell on all three axes.

  The prediction that run has to test, stated in advance so it can be wrong:
  the measured systematic term at those three control voltages is 0.8762 /
  0.8719 / 0.8206 ns, so a closed loop there is expected to settle **just
  inside** the ratified bound on systematic terms alone and **outside** it once
  the statistical term is present. Section 1's one cross-check of that kind of
  prediction — `typical`/−40 °C/3.63 V, predicted 0.8354 ns, measured 1.049 ns
  settled — came in 0.21 ns high, the size of the retiming term.
