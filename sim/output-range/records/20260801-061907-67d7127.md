    does; it is redundant, reproducible compute cost, not evidence of
    instability or nondeterminism. It is **not fixed in this record**
    (removing the redundant `run` changed convergence behavior on the RC
    sanity-check deck in an unrelated way that was not verified safe on the
    real DUT inside this session's budget), but is flagged as a real,
    bounded, likely ~2x throughput opportunity for whoever picks up #49's
    grid-extension scope next. **Separately**: the original #12 `lo` log's

  - **What the waveform shows** (see the archived
    `typical_27c_3.30v_lo_waveform.csv`, single clean pass, 1140 rows;
    `wrdata` only fires once, after the explicit `run`, so the file is not
    itself duplicated even though the underlying analysis is): from
    `vctrl_ic` release (`t_force` = 16 ns) to about `t` = 820 ns (~1 full
    reference period at `f_ref` = 1.25 MHz, `T_ref` = 800 ns), `up` and `dn`
    both sit within a few microvolts of 0 V with small ~1 uV/step
    alternating ripple -- a near-dead-zone PFD state, and `vctrl` correspondingly
    barely moves (1.67995 V at release -> 1.67969 V at 800 ns, essentially
    flat). At `t` = 820.2 ns (the second reference edge), `up` snaps cleanly
    to 3.3 V and **stays there continuously for the remainder of the 2 us
    window** while `dn` stays at its near-zero dead-zone-noise level -- a
    textbook **persistently one-sided PFD state**, which
    `design/README.md`'s own charge-pump section names explicitly as "what
    acquisition looks like." From that point, `vctrl` climbs smoothly and
    monotonically (1.68 V -> 2.11 V over the remaining ~1.18 us, roughly
    linear with a slight downward-curving rate as `vctrl` rises -- consistent
    with normal RC loop-filter integration, not a discontinuity or
    oscillation). There is **no jump, glitch, or discontinuity in `vctrl` at
    the `t_force` = 16 ns clamp-release instant itself** -- only a small
    (< 10 mV), fast-recovering dip, two orders of magnitude smaller than the
    eventual ~430 mV excursion that begins 800 ns later at the PFD's own
    reference-edge cadence, not at the release instant.
  - **Conclusion this waveform supports**: the `lo` edge's large `vctrl`
    movement is **real, correctly-signed, ordinary large-signal charge-pump
    acquisition current** -- `up` asserts persistently exactly when the loop
    needs to raise `vctrl` toward the true 10 MHz operating point, and does
    so at the PFD's normal reference-edge cadence, not at the clamp-release
    instant. This **rules out (a)** (no polarity error observed: the sign of
    `up`'s assertion matches the sign of `vctrl`'s subsequent movement) and
    **rules out (b)** (the movement begins 800 ns after release, at a
    reference edge, not at the release transient itself). **(c) does not
    apply to this edge** -- 2.1116 V is well inside the 0.9-2.7 V usable
    window, nowhere near a rail, so there is no rail-stability question to
    ask here (unlike `relock`'s 3.138 V reading -- see
    `sim/lock-time`'s own anomaly-investigation record, tracked separately
    under #49, for that case). This **confirms rather than overturns** the
    original record's own conclusion: `lo`'s reading is real but
    undersampled at only ~2.5 reference cycles, not evidence of a broken or
    unstable loop.
