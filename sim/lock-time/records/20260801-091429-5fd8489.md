  - **What the waveform shows** (`ss_125c_2.97v_n4_relock_waveform.csv`,
    8611 rows, single clean pass -- `wrdata` fires once, so the file is not
    itself duplicated by the double-analysis artifact above): from the
    very first sampled row (`t` = 0, immediately after the op-point solve,
    before the `t_force` = 1 ns clamp even releases), `dn` is already at
    2.97 V (rail) and stays there continuously -- unlike the `typical`
    corner's pilot, which showed a brief near-dead-zone period before its
    first real PFD decision, `ss`'s slower dynamics apparently let the
    op-point solver itself settle the PFD latches into an already-decided
    `dn`-asserted state. Measured directly off the full window: `dn` > 1.65 V
    for **80.25 %** of all 8611 sampled rows, `up` > 1.65 V for only
    **3.29 %** (20 discrete assertions, 19 of them ~2.5 ns blips; the one
    exception is an ~18 ns burst at 1021-1039 ns -- see the reversal
    below).
  - **`vctrl`'s trajectory -- net descent with a mid-window reversal**
    (all figures recomputed directly from the committed
    `ss_125c_2.97v_n4_relock_waveform.csv`): `vctrl` falls **overall** from
    the 2.7 V clamp value to **2.29983 V at `t` = 2 us**, ending 0.40 V
    below where it started and never once exceeding the 2.7 V clamp. The
    descent is **not monotonic**, however, and the record should not be
    read as claiming it is: of the 8610 sample-to-sample steps, **6602 are
    decreasing, 1991 are increasing** (17 flat), i.e. per-cycle charge-pump
    ripple is present throughout, and there is one **distinct ~214 mV
    reversal in the middle of the window**:
    - fast, clean, `dn`-dominated fall from 2.7 V to a local trough of
      **2.30618 V at `t` = 0.670 us** (100 ns-binned mean 2.3224 V in the
      0.6-0.7 us bin);
    - then a **rise** to a local peak of **2.52036 V at `t` = 1.061 us**
      (binned mean 2.4792 V in the 1.0-1.1 us bin, +0.157 V on the bin
      means), coincident with the only sustained `up` activity in the run:
      `up` duty rises to **17.3 %** in the 1.0-1.1 us bin against
      **at most 5.1 % in every other bin** -- the background maximum of
      5.1 % occurs in five bins (0.7-0.8, 0.9-1.0, 1.2-1.3, 1.3-1.4 and
      1.4-1.5 us), with 4.9 % in 0.0-0.1 us and 0-2.6 % in the remainder,
      so the burst bin is **~3.4x** the background maximum, not an
      order-of-magnitude outlier in duty terms. What distinguishes it is
      *continuity*, not duty alone: every one of those background bins is
      one or two isolated ~2.5 ns blips (11 samples each out of ~430),
      whereas the 1.0-1.1 us bin is a single sustained 18.20 ns assertion.
      `dn` duty falls to 42.3 % in that same bin. A plausible reading is a
      cycle-slip / re-acquisition episode -- the loop briefly pushing
      `vctrl` back up before resuming -- but this record does not have the
      per-edge phase data to prove that mechanism, and offers it only as a
      hypothesis for a later, dedicated run;
    - then a clean `dn`-dominated descent resumes (100 % `dn` duty across
      the 1.6-1.9 us bins) to the **minimum sampled value 2.24827 V at
      `t` = 1.9703 us** (row 8483 of 8611);
    - and finally `vctrl` **rises** over the last ~30 ns of the window,
      from that minimum back to 2.29983 V at `t` = 2 us -- a net +52 mV
      across the final 128 samples (102 of those 128 steps increasing),
      with a single ~2.5 ns `up` blip at 1971-1974 ns in that stretch.
      **So at the window's end the sampled trajectory is ascending, not
      descending**, on the ripple time scale; the *net* movement across
      the window is still downward, toward the target.
    There is no discontinuity or glitch in the trace -- the reversals above
    are smooth, loop-driven excursions, not numerical artifacts -- but
    direction reversals are unambiguously present, and the acquisition is
    clearly still in progress when the 2 us window closes.
  - **Conclusion this waveform supports, directly answering the open
    question from `eec269e`**: at `ss`/125 C/2.97 V, `relock`'s behavior is
    **not** the `up`-dominant, past-the-clamp rail excursion the `typical`
    corner showed -- it is the **opposite and textbook-expected** pattern:
    `dn` dominates (consistent with starting above the 80 MHz operating
    point, since at 2.7 V band 5 already runs well above 80 MHz at this
    corner too -- `sim/vco-tuning-range`'s own worst-margin-corner framing
    for `ff`/125 C/2.97 V does not directly give the `ss` Kvco number, but
    the same monotonic-band-vs-Vctrl relationship holds across process),
    and `vctrl` moves correctly toward the target rather than away from it
    (net across the window, and staying below the 2.7 V clamp throughout --
    the mid-window reversal described above is a ~214 mV excursion *inside*
    that net descent, not a departure from it, and is itself a reportable
    acquisition-dynamics feature rather than a contradiction of this
    conclusion).
    **This answers the `eec269e` open question at this one additional data
    point: the large-frequency-error anomaly is NOT a universal property of
    every corner at N=4/band 5/`relock`** -- it is corner-dependent, seen at
    `typical`/27 C/3.30 V and not seen (in this one additional sample) at
    `ss`/125 C/2.97 V. This is evidence toward, not proof of, a
    corner-dependent mechanism: **one additional corner is not the full
    grid**, and a mechanism that is absent at `ss` but present at `typical`
    could still recur at some of the 43 still-unsampled corners, including
    ones closer to `typical` than `ss` is. The FAIL status itself is
    unchanged from the pilot -- LOCK did not assert in either corner's 2 us
    window -- but the FAIL's *character* is materially different: at `ss`
    it is an ordinary "window too short, loop still converging correctly"
    FAIL (the same story the pilot's own `cold` row told), not the
    rail-excursion anomaly `relock` showed at `typical`.
