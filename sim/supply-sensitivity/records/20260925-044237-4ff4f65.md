- **Methodology / criteria / limitations**:
  - **The criteria are the ratified ones, not the deck's.** DR-012 Decision 5
    established that a testbench's acceptance threshold on a ratified quantity
    is the ratified value; this record applies the same rule to criterion 1b,
    which is the other place the campaign graded against a non-ratified
    number. Specifically:
    - **Budget 2** (`spec/pll.md` row 12, and the Supply sensitivity section's
      Budget 2): a DC rail excursion **over 2.97-3.63 V** must consume
      **<= 0.6 V** of the control window. Graded as `span_full_v`.
    - **The same budget over the excursion its derivation prices.** The
      derivation reads: "a +/-10 % (+/-0.33 V) rail excursion moves the
      open-loop frequency by up to 17 %. The loop cancels that by moving Vctrl
      by `0.17 / (Kvco/f_out)`, and `Kvco/f_out` spans 0.31 ... 0.84 per volt
      ... so the required Vctrl shift is **0.20 V ... 0.55 V**". Its **17 %**
      is `%/V x 0.33 V` -- which is exactly how the same section's pushing
      table computes its own "worst frequency shift over a +/-10 % rail"
      column (-50.7 %/V x 0.33 V = 16.7 %). So the derivation prices a
      **0.33 V** excursion while the row it supports specifies a **0.66 V**
      one. Graded as `span_half_v`, the worse of the two half-excursions.
      **This record reports both and decides neither.** Which excursion is
      normative changes whether the row is met at 9 of 15 cells, and choosing
      the one that passes would be relaxing a ratified line to make a result
      pass -- which CLAUDE.md forbids and this record does not do.
    - **The control window**, twice: DR-001 Decision 2's predicted
      0.9-2.4 V (what the source record's 1b check applied) and DR-003
      Decision 5's measured 0.9-2.7 V (which supersedes it and is what
      `spec/pll.md` now states). Both test the **ripple peaks**
      (`vctrl_min_v`/`vctrl_max_v`), not the average, because headroom is lost
      at the peak of the ripple -- the source record's own rule, kept so the
      two gradings are comparable.
    - **The frequency criteria** as the source record states them:
      `|fdev| <= 1000 ppm` and `|ferr| <= 1e-3`.
  - **The Budget-2 cross-check is an interpolation inside measured data, never
    an extrapolation.** For each cell it asks: at the band the loop actually
    ran in, what control voltage does 3.63 V need to produce the frequency
    that 2.97 V produces at the measured `vctrl_avg_v`? Both steps interpolate
    linearly inside the one bracketing interval of #8's measured 7-point
    `f(Vctrl)` curve at that (bundle, temperature, supply, band) -- the same
    rule the source record's own warm-start derivation used -- and a cell
    whose target falls outside the measured curve is emitted as `n/a` rather
    than extrapolated. None did.
  - **The recovery law is compared in volts, on the same samples, so the
    comparison is not an artefact of fitting one model in log space.** The
    control node is ripple-averaged in 0.5 us bins (the retained trace is
    decimated at 20 ns and carries the loop filter's own ripple, so one sample
    is not a position). The **final value** is the mean of the last 5.0 us of
    the hold; the node has **arrived** at the first bin within 20 mV of it;
    the **segment** is every bin before that instant. A straight line is fitted
    by least squares on `v` against `t`; a single exponential by least squares
    on `ln|v - final|` against `t` and then evaluated back in volts. Both
    residuals are RMS in volts. `better_fit` is the smaller.
  - **Two caveats on that comparison, stated rather than left to be found.**
    (a) At `ff`/125 C the segment is 4 bins spanning 20.4 mV, and the two
    residuals differ by 0.4 % (30.34 vs 30.45 mV): over a segment that short
    both models fit a nearly-straight piece of anything and `better_fit` does
    **not** discriminate there. The `segment_range_mv` and `n_fit_samples`
    columns exist so a reader can see that without re-deriving it. It does
    discriminate at the other two (37 bins / 425.5 mV and 19 bins / 307.5 mV).
    (b) Both residuals are of order 20-40 mV because the binned trace still
    carries ripple; they are a *relative* comparison between two models on
    identical samples, not an absolute goodness-of-fit.
  - **The model-free damping test depends on no fit at all**, which is why it
    is here: `overshoot_mv` is how far the ripple-averaged node goes **past**
    its own final value and `reversals` counts direction changes larger than
    20 mV over the whole post-ramp hold. An under-damped second-order recovery
    overshoots and reverses repeatedly; a current-limited slew approaches from
    one side and stops. `settled_ripple_pp_mv` is the control line's own
    peak-to-peak ripple over the settled window, and is the scale below which
    an "overshoot" is not an overshoot.
  - **`t_to_close_phase_us` is a rate, continued -- not a settling time.** In a
    type-II loop a residual fractional frequency error slips the REF->FB phase
    linearly at exactly df/f seconds per second, which is the identity
    `ferr` is defined by; so the phase outstanding at the hold's end divided by
    the slip rate measured there is the time to close it **at that rate**. A
    decaying response slows as it converges, so this is the **optimistic** end
    of a bracket. DR-011 declined to promote the equivalent quantity for the
    high plateau to a spec bound, on the strength of this same source record's
    section 3c, and nothing here changes that: the column is evidence about
    *which class* the END-plateau FAIL belongs to, not a number for a spec row.
  - **`decay_expected` is re-derived rather than trusted.** Section 3c's own
    column reads 0.04925 for the 28.0 us between its two holds; `exp(-dt/tau)`
    at the exact pole (tau = 9.313 us) is 0.049458, and 0.04925 is the same
    expression at a rounded tau = 9.3 us. The difference is 0.4 % of a
    quantity the classification compares against 0.809, so it changes no
    verdict -- checked here rather than assumed away.
