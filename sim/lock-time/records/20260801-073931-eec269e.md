  - **What the committed waveform shows** (`typical_27c_3.30v_n4_relock_waveform.csv`,
    8701 rows, single clean pass): from `vctrl_ic` release (`t_force` = 1 ns)
    to about `t` = 20 ns, `dn` sits at 3.3 V (a pre-reference-edge PFD state
    -- REF's own first real pulse does not fire until `t` = 20 ns per the
    deck's `pulse(... 20n ...)` stimulus, so this reflects the op-point
    solver settling the PFD's internal latches into a state driven by the
    VCO already free-running from `t` = 0, not a real REF/FB phase
    comparison), and `vctrl` correspondingly dips slightly, 2.700 V -> a
    minimum of **2.6565 V at t = 20.2 ns**. This dip is small (< 45 mV) and
    fast, and it is in the **correct, expected direction for `dn`**: a
    directly observable confirmation that the charge pump's own `dn`->
    `vctrl`-falls / `up`->`vctrl`-rises mapping is correctly signed at this
    operating point (ruling out a bare CP output-polarity wiring error
    outright, independent of everything below).
  - **From `t` = 20.5 ns onward, `up` asserts and then stays HIGH for the
    overwhelming majority of the remaining ~1.98 us window**: measured
    directly off the committed CSV, `up` > 1.65 V for **86.48 %** of all
    8701 sampled rows across the full window, while `dn` > 1.65 V for only
    **1.57 %** (a handful of brief, isolated re-assertions near `t` = 345 ns,
    795-797 ns, 1421 ns, and 1646 ns, each lasting well under 1 ns before
    `up` reasserts and dominates again). Checked at full simulator time
    resolution (not just the coarse samples quoted above) inside
    representative 100 ns windows (e.g. `t` in [100 ns, 200 ns]): `up` never
    dips below the 1.65 V logic threshold even once in that span -- this is
    a genuinely sustained, not merely duty-cycle-averaged-and-aliased, HIGH
    state. `vctrl` climbs smoothly and monotonically alongside it, from
    2.661 V at `t` = 20.5 ns to 3.138207 V at `t` = 2 us, with no
    discontinuity, oscillation, or turnaround visible anywhere in the window
    -- the excursion is still growing, not saturating, when the window ends.
  - **This alone would read exactly like `lo`'s finding** (a textbook
    "persistently one-sided PFD state," `design/README.md`'s own name for
    ordinary acquisition) **-- except for one fact `lo`'s situation did not
    have to contend with**, established with a **supplementary, uncommitted
    diagnostic run**: a hand-modified copy of `tb_lock_time.sp` (kept only
    under `/tmp`, never staged -- same "probe deck, not committed evidence"
    pattern `sim/output-range`'s `lo` record used for its RC sanity-check
    deck) that additionally `wrdata`s `v(clk)` (the VCO output) and `v(fb)`
    (the divider's retimed feedback edge, the PFD's own second input). Run
    against the identical corner/params, it reproduced `vctrl_final` =
    3.138207 V **bit-for-bit** (confirming the extra probe nodes did not
    perturb the circuit), and shows:
    - **The VCO is not stalled.** `clk` continues to oscillate vigorously
      throughout the window (full rail-to-rail transitions every few ns,
      consistent with a many-tens-of-MHz oscillation) -- ruling out a "VCO
      bias pushed out of its valid range by the rising `vctrl` and stopped
      oscillating, freezing `fb` and leaving the PFD latched on stale state"
      explanation, which was the leading candidate for why the observed
      behavior might diverge from `lo`'s.
    - **`fb` toggles cleanly and, critically, FASTER than `ref`.** Reading
      consecutive `fb` rising edges in the `t` = [100 ns, 300 ns] window
      gives periods of 31.4, 30.8, 30.4, 29.8, 29.6, 29.2 ns -- an
      instantaneous `fb` frequency of **~31.9-34.2 MHz**, smoothly
      increasing as `vctrl` climbs (consistent with the VCO's own positive,
      still-monotonic `Kvco` at these voltages: `sim/vco-tuning-range`'s own
      `kvco_by_point.csv` measures band 5 at Vctrl = 2.70 V, typical/27 C/
      3.30 V as `fosc` = 124.434 MHz, i.e. `fb` = 124.434/4 = **31.11 MHz**
      at the clamp's own release voltage -- matching this window's
      measurement almost exactly, and confirming the open-loop
      characterization's number is what this closed-loop transient is
      actually doing, not an extrapolation artifact). `ref`, by construction
      of the deck's `pulse(...)` source, is fixed at 20 MHz (period 50 ns).
      **`fb` is therefore running ~1.6-1.7x FASTER than `ref` throughout
      this window, and getting faster, not slower, as `vctrl` climbs.**
    - **This is the opposite of what the persistent `up` assertion should
      mean.** `design/pfd.sch`'s own header comment describes the circuit
      plainly: each input's rising edge SETs its own NAND SR latch (REF's
      edge sets the latch driving `up`; FB's edge sets the latch driving
      `dn`), and both latches share one RESET generated as `AND(up, dn)`
      through a 24-inverter delay chain. Under that description, a `fb` that
      arrives *more often* than `ref` should assert (and keep re-asserting)
      **`dn`**, not `up` -- `dn`'s latch would SET on the very next `fb`
      edge after every reset and simply stay set, since a level-latched SR
      latch does not un-set itself on additional redundant SET pulses,
      waiting only for `ref`'s comparatively rare edges to also fire before
      the shared reset can clear both. That is exactly backwards from what
      is measured: `dn` essentially never asserts (1.57 % of the window)
      while `fb` keeps arriving on schedule and cleanly (no glitching or
      missing edges visible in the probe), and `up` dominates instead.
  - **Conclusion this waveform (and its supplementary probe) supports**:
    `relock`'s anomaly is **not** ordinary correctly-signed large-signal
    acquisition current like `lo`'s -- it **rules out (a)** in the narrow
    "wrong CP output polarity" sense (the CP's own `up`/`dn` -> `vctrl`
    mapping is directly confirmed correct via the initial `dn`-driven dip)
    but the PFD's *decision* about which of `up`/`dn` to assert, given the
    measured REF/FB edge relationship, is the opposite of the naive
    tri-state-PFD expectation for "FB faster than REF" -- a genuine,
    reproducible large-frequency-error response anomaly, not a simple
    polarity bug. It **rules out (b)**: the divergence from a clean
    release-instant transient begins at `t` = 20.5 ns (the very first real
    REF edge) and is sustained by ongoing, correctly-clocked REF/FB activity
    for the full remaining ~1.98 us, not a `SWFORCE` switching glitch at
    `t_force` itself. It **rules out (d)** for the same reason
    `sim/output-range`'s companion record already established (the "double
    analysis" signature is an independently-confirmed general ngspice
    batch-mode artifact, unrelated to either anomaly). **(c) does apply
    here, unlike `lo`** -- but not in the originally-anticipated
    "large-signal effect *at* the rail" sense; the mechanism is a real
    **large-frequency-error PFD/CP acquisition-dynamics anomaly**: when the
    control-node disturbance this "worst-case re-lock" scenario forces
    (`vctrl_ic` = 2.7 V) puts the VCO far enough above the target frequency
    that `fb` runs at ~1.6-1.7x `ref`'s rate, the closed loop's actual
    dynamic response pushes `vctrl` **further away** from the target rather
    than back toward it -- a positive-feedback, divergent regime at large
    initial frequency error, not the negative-feedback, dead-zone-limited
    regime `pfd-deadzone` and `cp-compliance` characterize (both of those
    campaigns measure the PFD/CP's behavior *near zero phase error*; neither
    exercises a multiple-FB-edges-per-REF-period condition like this one).
    This genuinely is a materially different situation from `lo`'s, exactly
    as #65 warned it might be -- not a recycled conclusion.
  - **What this record does NOT resolve, stated plainly**: the
    transistor-level reason the PFD's SR-latch structure fails to assert
    `dn` despite `fb` continuing to arrive cleanly and on-schedule needs
    visibility into the PFD's own internal SR-latch/reset nodes (not
    `up`/`dn` themselves, which are the latches' *outputs*), which neither
    this record's committed instrumentation nor its supplementary probe
    captures -- that is out of scope for a waveform-reading exercise built
    on the existing `vctrl`/`up`/`dn`/`lock`/`vwin` capture and is flagged
    as a genuine open item, not silently inferred. Also not established:
    whether this is specific to N=4/band 5/this exact corner, or a more
    general property of the PFD circuit under any sufficiently large
    FB-faster-than-REF condition -- answering that needs either a dedicated
    large-frequency-error PFD acquisition testbench (bypassing the VCO/
    divider, driving REF/FB directly at a swept frequency ratio) or the
    grid-extension runs #65 itself asks for, read with this same lens.
  - **Reliability implication, stated as a risk, not re-litigated as a new
    claim here**: if this mechanism holds more generally, a control-node
    disturbance large enough to push `vctrl` into a regime where `fb`
    substantially outruns `ref` may not be a recoverable transient at all --
    the loop's own dynamics could drive it further from lock rather than
    back toward it, matching the original record's own description of
    `vctrl_final` landing "past the 2.7 V clamp and the entire usable
    window" rather than converging back inside it. This is exactly the kind
    of finding #65's own gate ("do not extend ... until this is understood")
    exists to catch before spending many more corner-hours reproducing the
    same unexplained anomaly forty-five more times.
